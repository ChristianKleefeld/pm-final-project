# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Variable-income consumers with two or more claims (about 34% of journey starters have variable or shift income).
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To set up a payment plan they can realistically keep, so the collection letters stop and the debt doesn’t grow.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** At the income question in the affordability step, they can’t tell what “income” means average month / last month, and they fear a wrong number will be used against them (N-01, N-06). The portal doesn’t explain why income is asked or what happens after a missed payment (T-206, T-208), so they either close it “meaning to come back” or guess, pick the plan duration that “looked like the right answer,” and miss the second payment (N-02) if the installment rate is too high.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** The interviews show three behavioural workarounds, all of which happen inside or around the portal: abandoning and returning, guessing and choosing by heuristic, and seeking a human or chat check.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** > Add up the debt by hand. The portal shows each claim separately (T-202, N-03), so the consumer collects the amounts from letters and the portal and totals them. They may also compare portal and letter figures where these differ (N-07, T-203).
> Work out a “normal” monthly income. Because the portal doesn’t say whether “income” means this month or a typical month (N-01, T-206), they would need to look through bank statements or payslips to average out irregular pay.
> Call an agent to confirm. They ask whether the plan will cost them more and what happens if they miss a payment (N-04), then repeat details they already typed online (N-08).
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The income question in the affordability step. It asks for income without saying whether it means this month or a normal month, why it is needed or how long it is kept (N-01, T-206), and the consumer fears the number will be used against them: ‘What if they use it against me?’ (N-06)
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Primary quote (N-01, consumer, two open claims, shift work):
“I opened it on my break. It asked for my income and I did not know if they meant this month or a normal month. I closed it and meant to come back.”

Supporting quote (N-06, consumer, three open claims, said finances are tight):
“I was scared to type the wrong number. What if they use it against me?”

This covers the second half of the friction, fear of misuse. Note that N-06 is not described as a shift worker, so it supports the friction but not the segment.

Supporting behaviour (N-02, consumer, one claim):
“I picked the plan with the lowest number of months because it looked like the right answer. The amount was too high and I missed the second payment.”

This shows the downstream result of guessing under uncertainty. N-02 is also not described as a shift worker.

N-03 quote as direct multi-claim evidence: “I could only see one claim at a time. I did not know if paying one would stop the letters about the other.”
